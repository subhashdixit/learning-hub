## 01 — Scarcity, opportunity cost, incentives and marginal decisions

[Read the lesson](../01-theory/lesson-01-economic-reasoning.md)

### Q001. Opportunity versus accounting cost?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Opportunity cost includes the best forgone use, including noncash resources; accounting cost records defined expenses.

</details>

### Q002. Why ignore sunk cost in a new comparison?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

It cannot be changed by the current choice, though future consequences still matter.

</details>

### Q003. What is a marginal decision?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Compare the additional benefit and cost of the next increment, not only historical averages.

</details>

## 02 — Supply, demand, equilibrium, elasticity and market structure

[Read the lesson](../01-theory/lesson-02-markets-and-elasticity.md)

### Q004. Shift versus movement along a demand curve?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A price change moves along a held-fixed demand relationship; other demand determinants shift that relationship.

</details>

### Q005. Does negative correlation identify elasticity?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No. Causal price response requires controlling/identifying the relevant changes.

</details>

### Q006. Why multiply revenue factors?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Revenue is price×quantity; simultaneous percentage changes compound multiplicatively.

</details>

## 03 — GDP, inflation, real values, rates and economic data

[Read the lesson](../01-theory/lesson-03-macro-measurement.md)

### Q007. Disinflation versus deflation?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Disinflation is slower positive price growth; deflation is a falling price level over the stated period.

</details>

### Q008. Stock versus flow?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

A stock is measured at a time; a flow over an interval.

</details>

### Q009. Why preserve data vintages?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

They identify what was available then rather than substituting later revised information.

</details>

## 04 — Cash flows, discounting, NPV and financial statements

[Read the lesson](../01-theory/lesson-04-cashflows-and-statements.md)

### Q010. Why discount?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

To compare cash flows at different dates under an explicit time/risk valuation convention.

</details>

### Q011. Profit equals cash?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No. Accrual timing, working capital and other accounting items create differences.

</details>

### Q012. Does positive NPV guarantee success?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No. It is conditional on forecasts, timing and the chosen discount-rate assumptions.

</details>

## 05 — Compounding, volatility, drawdown, diversification and factor ideas

[Read the lesson](../01-theory/lesson-05-returns-and-risk.md)

### Q013. Why does +10% then−10% not cancel?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The second percentage applies to a different base; the factors multiply to.99.

</details>

### Q014. Is maximum drawdown a future bound?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No. It summarizes the observed historical path.

</details>

### Q015. Does low volatility mean no risk?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No. Illiquidity, default, concentration, model and tail risks may remain.

</details>

## 06 — Backtesting, costs, leakage, selection bias and model risk

[Read the lesson](../01-theory/lesson-06-backtest-and-risk-controls.md)

### Q016. What is look-ahead bias?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Using information unavailable at the simulated decision time.

</details>

### Q017. Why include turnover costs?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Small per-trade costs can overwhelm a frequently traded small gross edge.

</details>

### Q018. Does temporal holdout remove all bias?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

No. Data vintages, survivorship, execution assumptions and selection still require audit.

</details>
