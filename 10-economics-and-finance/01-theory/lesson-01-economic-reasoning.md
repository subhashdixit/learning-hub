## The idea

**Analogy:** Opportunity cost is the next-best road you could have taken, not just the cash paid on the chosen road.

Scarcity means resources cannot satisfy every desired use. A decision uses time, money, attention or capacity that could support another option. Opportunity cost is the value of the best forgone alternative. Sunk costs have already been incurred and cannot be recovered; they should not determine a forward-looking comparison unless they affect future consequences.

Marginal analysis compares the incremental benefit and incremental cost of one more unit/action. Incentives change behaviour, sometimes in unintended ways. A target such as “maximize tickets closed”can encourage easy-ticket selection rather than better service. Constraints, externalities and distributional effects matter beyond one organization's private objective.

Economic models simplify behaviour under assumptions. Distinguish a positive claim about what happens from a normative judgement about what should happen. An observed association can reflect selection, confounding or simultaneous decisions rather than a causal response. Use the model to frame a measurable question rather than as an unquestionable law.

## Work through it

A study session takes 2 hours. If the best alternative is paid work worth 200 per hour, the illustrative opportunity cost of time is 400, even if no money is spent on the session. The learning benefit may still justify it; the calculation makes the comparison explicit.

A project already spent 1000. If the next step costs 100 and produces expected value 50 under stated assumptions, the past 1000 does not make the next step attractive.

## Common mistakes and practical limits

Expected value is not a guaranteed realized return. Monetary values may not capture all learning/social benefits. Do not label all fixed costs sunk; a future avoidable fixed cost can still matter.
